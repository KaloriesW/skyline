SKYLINE / TERRAINWATCH
Team-trained air-quality forecasting and landslide segmentation for Taiwan | Climate track

OUR CONTRIBUTION
We trained two AI systems during this hackathon: city-specific PM2.5 and PM10 forecasting models, and a fine-tuned multispectral landslide segmentation model. We integrated their actual predictions into Skyline / TerrainWatch, a Taiwan weather and satellite-review application. The aim is to help users anticipate poor air quality and prioritize suspected landslides for human review.

1. WE TRAINED PM2.5 AND PM10 FORECASTING MODELS
Our teammate built the air-quality training pipeline and trained separate next-day PM2.5 and PM10 predictors for each of Taiwan's 22 cities/counties. Targets are tomorrow's daily-average concentrations in micrograms per cubic metre. Training uses historical CAMS air-quality estimates and ERA5 weather obtained through Open-Meteo. Candidate feature sets combine recent pollution, weather and seasonal variables; expanded sets add 3/7-day pollution trends and four upwind locations. We fitted ridge regression, gradient-boosted trees and their blend, selecting a model separately for each city and pollutant through four time-based cross-validation folds. Taichung has 1,002 training days and 365 test days, separated by a one-day gap. The selected models are exported to JSON and used by the app's Tomorrow's Air card; this card reports our trained predictions.

2. WE TRAINED AND FINE-TUNED THE LANDSLIDE MODEL
We trained an earlier U-Net on Landslide4Sense, then adapted and fine-tuned a ResNet34 U-Net using Taiwan Ministry of Agriculture landslide-inventory labels. The retained model takes 13 inputs: 11 Sentinel-2 spectral bands, including infrared, plus terrain elevation and slope. Training used AdamW (learning rate 0.0001), weighted BCE plus Dice loss, and rotations/flips. We retained epoch 5 and selected threshold 0.024 on pilot validation. The submitted app loads this trained checkpoint and processes 128 x 128 windows to produce mapped candidate polygons. Earlier Ultralytics YOLO experiments and confidence sweeps are included as supporting evidence, not relabelled as U-Net results.

MEASURED RESULTS
Taichung PM2.5: test MAE 4.38 vs 5.91 micrograms/m3 for a tomorrow-equals-today baseline (25.9% lower error). PM10: 4.86 vs 6.04 micrograms/m3 (19.5% lower). Both use the same 365-day test period and the cross-validation-selected models.
Landslides: 54.27% pixel IoU and 70.36% Dice, with 62.13% precision and 81.09% recall, on 24 inventory-labelled patches in three regions. The frozen transferred comparator scored 43.15% IoU on that same holdout. These results are not a direct comparison with YOLO's different evaluation sets.

WORKING APPLICATION
Users scan Taiwan satellite imagery, inspect exact candidate locations, compare before/after vegetation and review slope, road proximity and forecast rain. Optional monitoring checks for new imagery and saves hourly reports. Whole-Taiwan mode provides a resumable queue and experimental rain-informed review priorities; a full national pass has not been completed. We integrated an existing local Qwen model for evidence-based follow-up questions; we did not train Qwen.

LIMITATIONS AND CREDITS
CAMS targets are model estimates, not station measurements; historical weather evaluation is not a prospective forecast trial. The landslide holdout was repeatedly consulted during development, and live Taiwan accuracy remains unmeasured. Candidates and rain-informed priorities are not confirmed hazards or validated predictions of slope failure. Next steps: station-based air-quality evaluation and independent annotation of recent Taiwan scenes.
