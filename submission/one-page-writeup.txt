SKYLINE / TERRAIN WATCH
Taiwan weather, air-quality forecasts and satellite landslide review | Climate track | Live: https://veritas.premiersalesri.com

PROBLEM AND PURPOSE
After heavy rain, Taiwan's mountains shed landslides that block roads, and on many days the air in its western cities is unhealthy. Weather apps show neither. Skyline is a weather app for Taiwan whose centrepiece, TerrainWatch, finds suspected landslides in free satellite imagery, and whose own models forecast tomorrow's PM2.5 so schools can plan outdoor PE.

WHAT WE BUILT AND HOW
TerrainWatch (landslides). We fine-tuned a ResNet34 U-Net on 11 Sentinel-2 bands plus elevation and slope (13 inputs), starting from our Landslide4Sense experiment and training on the Taiwan Ministry of Agriculture landslide inventory (AdamW, BCE + Dice loss, flips and rotations; threshold 0.024 chosen on a pilot validation set). For any area the app fetches and stitches imagery, masks cloud, shadow and water, runs 128-pixel windows and turns the mask into map polygons with coordinates. Before/after imagery, vegetation change, slope, nearby roads and rain context help a reviewer decide what to inspect. A local Qwen model proposes scan areas and answers questions from the scan evidence. Optional monitoring rescans an area when a new image is published.
PM2.5 forecasts. One next-day PM2.5 and PM10 model per city for all 22 Taiwan cities and counties. Inputs: today's and yesterday's pollution, 3- and 7-day averages, today's weather, tomorrow's forecast weather, pollution at four upwind points and how hard tomorrow's north wind pushes it south. For each city, cross-validation over the last training year chose between ridge regression, boosted trees and a blend; the last 365 days were scored once as a held-out test year. The bad-air warning threshold was tuned for schools, where a missed bad day is worse than a false alarm. Models are exported to JSON so the server needs no ML libraries.
The app. Weather home page with live Open-Meteo data for any location, the tomorrow's-air forecast and a school outdoor-PE plan, Predictions maps (satellite and Windy), CWA typhoon tracks, an assistant grounded only in the data on screen, Chinese and English, and honest "not connected" states instead of placeholder numbers. A teammate's Apple-Weather-style interface and Taichung model were merged into it.

MEASURED RESULTS
PM2.5, held-out test year (2025-11-08 to 2026-11-07): every city beats the "tomorrow = today" baseline, with 18% to 41% lower error. Taichung: 4.38 vs 5.91 ug/m3 (26% lower); the tuned warning caught 139 of 143 days above 35 ug/m3, with 91 false alarms.
Landslides, 24 inventory patches in three regions: 54.3% pixel IoU and 70.4% Dice (precision 62.1%, recall 81.1%), versus 43.2% IoU for the frozen transferred model. A live 6 x 6 km Taroko scan took about a minute and gave 76 candidates; filters for road distance and size narrowed them to 5 review priorities.

LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEP
The landslide patches were compared repeatedly during development, so they are not an untouched test set, and live-image accuracy is unmeasured; candidates are suspected, not confirmed. Our landslide comparison is against another deep model, not a simple rule. PM2.5 targets are CAMS model estimates, not station readings, and the school warning trades misses for false alarms. Next: independently annotate recent Taiwan scenes, compare against a simple vegetation-loss-on-steep-slope rule, and retrain PM2.5 on Ministry of Environment station data.
